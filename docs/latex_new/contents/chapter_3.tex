The design of Mneme follows a continuous research activity. A researcher repeatedly encounters new literature, decides what deserves attention, reconstructs what has already been read, checks evidence, and revises an evolving account of personal interests. Chapter~2 establishes the customer evidence behind this activity. The present chapter translates that evidence into the application's story map, acceptance boundaries, engine architecture, interface contract, and interaction design. It also reports the formative usability study that tested whether the principal workflow was legible in an interactive prototype.

Traceability is a cross-cutting design property. Paper-derived artifacts are associated with an observed source revision; generated answers are constrained to retrieved excerpts from one paper; behavioral adaptation separates positive and negative evidence and calibrates its influence by evidence confidence; and mobile states distinguish live, cached, controlled, degraded, and failed outcomes. Together, these mechanisms make the origin and evidential status of an output available to the surrounding software and, where useful, to the reader. Their behavior and output quality are examined empirically in Chapter~4. The design connects the representation, evidence, interest, and interaction challenges introduced in Chapter~1.

\section{Storymap and Features}
\label{sec:storymap-features}

The customer research revealed complementary needs before and after a paper is selected. Participants used abstracts, figures, venues, authors, and code availability as rapid triage signals. They also needed to recover and verify information after the initial encounter. The resulting workflow connects discovery, interpretation, evidence inspection, retention, and renewed discovery. The story map in Figure~\ref{fig:storymap} expresses this dependency as five stages in the research loop.

The first stage, receiving new papers, is deliberately an engine-facing stage because every later interaction depends on the quality of the source representation. Mneme observes metadata from scholarly providers, associates each observation with a logical work and a specific revision, acquires the corresponding PDF where permitted, and produces a structured or explicitly degraded representation. A paper that cannot be parsed confidently remains available through its metadata and abstract. This fallback preserves continuity and prevents an abstract-only representation from being mistaken for full-text evidence.

Learning interests combines direct instruction with observed interaction. A first-use flow asks for research topics and a seed paper, which gives the system a useful starting point without requiring a long preference form. The mobile client records paper impressions as exposure evidence and records opens and paper-scoped questions as supported positive observations; each event can be uploaded with duplicate-safe identity. The shared event contract further assigns semantics to explicit save, share, and exposed-skip actions without interpreting the absence of an action as preference evidence. The design keeps explicit topics and the contrastive behavioral profile distinguishable because a chosen topic and an inferred signal have different epistemic meanings. The user interface can consequently offer recommendation reasons and correction controls without presenting behavioral inference as certain intent.

Planning what to read turns the candidate stream into a research briefing. The default product rhythm is weekly, while an explicit refresh can request an on-demand recommendation snapshot. Each entry contains the paper identity, a concise description, a normalized relevance score, and a reason derived from the scoring components. The briefing is an immutable snapshot: later preference changes affect subsequent briefings and leave the rationale for an earlier recommendation unchanged. This property supports reproducibility and also gives the mobile cache a stable object to restore.

The source-grounded reading stage supports two complementary tasks: structured summarization and paper-specific question answering. The summary separates a concise account of the paper from its claims, method, and limitations. Question answering addresses a narrower information need by retrieving excerpts from one observed revision and asking a language model to answer from that evidence. The interface presents source labels with an answer and uses an insufficient-evidence state when the retrieved excerpts cannot support a response. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) generally combines parametric generation with an explicit non-parametric memory~\cite{lewis2020rag}; Mneme narrows that memory to the selected paper revision so that the evidence set remains inspectable.

Exploring connections uses a bounded directed citation graph. The central paper, locally known citing papers, and locally known referenced papers form a neighborhood that can be traversed without loading an unbounded scholarly network onto a phone. The response represents the locally resolved neighborhood and records whether algorithmic enrichment or a deterministic citation-only fallback produced its ordering. Unresolved external identities remain available for later resolution. Selection leads to a native preview and a persistent action that returns the reader to a paper detail screen, so graph exploration remains part of the reading task.

The five stages form a closed loop. Receiving and preparation make evidence available; explicit and observed interests make ranking possible; briefing interactions identify the next paper; source-grounded reading creates further observations; and graph traversal discovers additional works that may re-enter the preparation pipeline. The architecture must therefore preserve identity and failure information across repeated cycles. That requirement explains why the following acceptance criteria include provenance, idempotency, boundedness, and degraded operation in addition to visible screen outcomes.

Several criteria specify how the system presents partial but useful outcomes. An abstract-only representation, an insufficient-evidence answer, a fallback graph, and a cached briefing remain useful when their origin is visible. This principle gives the API and client a consistent vocabulary: quality, source match, algorithm status, processing state, and content origin are part of the feature contract. Acceptance therefore depends on the meaning of the displayed state as well as the presence of content.

The criteria also distinguish repeatable software behavior from empirical output quality. A duplicate-safe event or an idempotent document job can be accepted because its state transition is deterministic, whereas the relevance of a recommended paper and the usefulness of a generated answer require empirical evaluation. An evidence-based refusal is the correct Q\&A result when retrieved support is inadequate, and an abstract-only paper is the correct ingestion result when the source cannot support full-text processing. This distinction allows Chapter~4 to examine software correctness and empirical quality with measures suited to each.

\section{Engine Architecture}
\label{sec:engine-architecture}

\subsection{System Boundaries and End-to-End Flow}

Mneme separates the latency-sensitive mobile experience from document processing and model inference. Figure~\ref{fig:system-architecture} shows four operational regions. The Android application owns presentation, navigation, explicitly labelled local state, and durable capture of supported interactions. The FastAPI service owns authentication, validation, public state transitions, and orchestration. PostgreSQL with pgvector owns durable domain and vector state, while Redis and ARQ carry replaceable execution attempts and cache entries. Scholarly sources and model providers remain external dependencies whose responses are validated before they affect durable state.

The model contains two kinds of change. Paper metadata and user preference are evolving state: a later observation can refresh the current catalogue view, and a later explicit edit or event replay can produce a new preference state. Revisions, generated artifacts, briefing snapshots, accepted events, and conversation messages are historical records. A new revision or briefing creates a new object and preserves the basis of earlier output. This separation permits the system to answer both a current-state question, such as ``what should be read next?'', and a provenance question, such as ``which paper text supported this earlier answer?''.

Historical records retain stable references and the fields needed to interpret their original state. A briefing entry stores its rank, score, reason, paper identity, and the preference and generator versions needed to interpret the snapshot. It obtains current bibliographic metadata from the catalogue. A Q\&A conversation is scoped to one paper, and its assistant messages retain source records for the revision used at generation time. A behavioral event carries a paper identity when the event semantics require one. This design keeps mutable paper and profile objects out of historical records and enables referential validation at each service boundary.

The Android region follows a unidirectional state flow. A screen emits an action to its ViewModel, the ViewModel obtains a domain result through a repository, and the result becomes a typed UI state observed by Compose. Retrofit and OkHttp map the versioned API into transport objects, while repositories reconcile remote responses with Room snapshots before presentation. Android's offline-first guidance similarly assigns coordination of local and network sources to the data layer and recommends an immediately readable local source for supported offline behavior~\cite{androidOfflineFirst2026}. Mneme applies this pattern to briefing and paper snapshots. This boundary prevents a network response from becoming interface state directly and allows controlled and live data sources to exercise the same ViewModel and rendering path. Loading, content, processing, cached, and error conditions are represented explicitly in the state model.

Stable paper identifiers connect the briefing, paper detail, paper-scoped Q\&A, and citation graph without serializing a complete paper object into a navigation route; source records retain the same paper identity when the reader inspects supporting material. DataStore retains small preference and mode values, while Room retains briefing and paper metadata together with a duplicate-safe event queue. WorkManager is intended for persistent Android work that must survive loss of the visible screen or process and provides retry policies for failed work~\cite{androidWorkManager2026}. Mneme uses it for constrained event upload and scheduled briefing refresh, while screen-bound reads remain cancellable coroutines. A locally packaged WebView asset renders the graph, and node identity is mirrored through a restricted JavaScript bridge into a native Compose preview. The graph selection is saved against its centre-paper route, so returning from an opened paper restores the prior graph context. This arrangement preserves the interaction benefits of a force-directed graph while keeping navigation and accessibility-relevant selection state in the native layer.

The server region is intentionally divided between synchronous request handling and asynchronously recoverable work. Catalogue reads, preference updates, event ingestion, recommendation scoring, and bounded graph assembly operate through domain services and repositories. PDF acquisition, parsing, summarization, chunking, embedding, and scheduled briefing assembly use durable jobs and ARQ workers because their latency and external dependencies do not fit a single mobile request. The API can therefore return either a complete representation or an accepted job identity without holding a connection open during document preparation.

PostgreSQL is the source of truth because papers, revisions, citations, preferences, generated artifacts, and job histories must survive queue eviction and process restart. pgvector resides in the same database so that vector retrieval can be filtered by the relational paper and revision identities in one query. Redis provides low-latency completion caching, budget counters, and ARQ delivery; durable completion is determined from the database. Local file storage holds PDF and parsed sidecar artifacts under UUID-derived paper and revision directories. This choice is sufficient for the study deployment and can be replaced by object storage if later use requires a different storage scale.

The external region is an untrusted input boundary. arXiv metadata and PDF URLs must agree on a logical identifier and explicit revision. Semantic Scholar observations can refer to papers outside the local catalogue, so unresolved endpoint identities are retained without being exposed as fabricated local nodes. Model responses are parsed, associated with provider and prompt metadata, and checked against output contracts before persistence. These boundaries allow a provider failure to become a controlled error or degraded artifact without corrupting the logical paper history.

The principal architectural alternative was a phone-centred design in which document extraction and language-model inference execute on the device. That approach would reduce server dependence but would impose model, memory, battery, and background-execution constraints unrelated to the main research questions. Mneme assigns interaction continuity to the mobile client and places replaceable computational services behind a stable API. A single synchronous document-processing request was also considered. Partial failure in that design would restart the entire pipeline and obscure which revision and stage produced an artifact. The staged architecture trades additional state for recovery, provenance, and bounded retries.

These invariants also determine where validation occurs. Provider data is checked before persistence, persisted identities are checked before expensive work, model outputs are checked before publication, and transport objects are checked before they become mobile domain models. Validation is repeated at boundaries because each layer protects a different claim. A valid PDF byte stream may still yield weak document structure; a valid JSON graph may still contain a dangling edge; a valid source marker may still provide inadequate support for a claim. The system records these outcomes separately so that one successful check does not erase a later limitation.

Failure handling follows the ownership of state. A worker may retry a replaceable execution attempt, while the database retains the durable job and any earlier valid artifact. The mobile client may retry a request, while its local cache retains the last disclosed snapshot. A model call may be repeated only when its canonical task identity and budget policy permit it. This division reduces the number of components that can declare an operation complete and makes recovery behavior a first-class part of the design.

\subsection{Revision-Aware Document and Evidence Flow}

The data model distinguishes a logical paper \(p\) from an observed revision \(r\). The logical paper retains discovery metadata and stable external identifiers; the revision retains the source checksum, byte size, parser version, parse checksum, quality tier, and timestamps associated with one content state. A derived artifact \(a\) is therefore interpreted as
\begin{equation}
  a = f\!\left(p,r,h,\theta\right).
  \label{eq:artifact-lineage}
\end{equation}
In Equation~\ref{eq:artifact-lineage}, \(h\) is the content checksum and \(\theta\) contains the transformation identity relevant to the artifact, such as parser, embedding model, prompt, or generator configuration. Equality of the logical paper identifier does not imply that two artifacts are interchangeable. Reuse is valid only when the material lineage fields for the artifact class match.

The arXiv synchronization path first normalizes the provider identifier, then parses and validates an entire Atom page before persistence. Metadata for a logical work is refreshed only when the provider exposes a newer source timestamp, while a previously unseen revision creates a distinct version row. Network access occurs outside the database transaction, and the validated page is persisted atomically. A serial request limiter and bounded retry policy for transport, rate-limit, and server errors prevent concurrent background tasks from turning provider pressure into inconsistent local state.

Figure~\ref{fig:document-dag} shows the resulting processing directed acyclic graph. Download, parse, summary, chunk, and embedding stages carry both the logical paper UUID and the exact revision UUID. Parsing is the branch point: a structured document can feed summary generation in parallel with deterministic chunk construction, and chunk construction subsequently feeds embedding. Readiness is reconciled only from artifacts for the selected revision.

Each stage receives an idempotency key computed from canonical JSON containing the stage, pipeline version, scope, and material inputs. Download identity includes the paper and revision; parse identity additionally includes the source checksum and parser version; embedding identity includes the parsed artifact and embedding model. A uniqueness constraint causes concurrent submissions of the same logical work to converge on one durable job. The database records queued, running, succeeded, or failed state and a safe public error code. Redis receives an attempt identifier derived from the durable job and the next dispatch identity, while the worker increments the persisted attempt only when execution begins. A stale dispatch lease can be recovered without changing the durable job UUID.

Document storage applies the same all-or-nothing principle to bytes. The downloader validates the URL, declared and streamed size, PDF signature, and checksum. It writes to a temporary file, synchronizes the bytes, and atomically replaces the destination. Parsed sidecars use the same publication rule and include schema version, paper and revision identifiers, source checksum, page count, ordered sections, page ranges, quality tier, fallback reason, and timestamp. After a crash, storage contains either the complete previous artifact or an identifiable temporary file; an incomplete file cannot appear as a valid published artifact.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
  \caption{Revision-safe document preparation}
  \label{alg:document-preparation}
  \KwIn{Logical paper \(p\), revision \(r\), validated source metadata, pipeline configuration \(\theta\)}
  \KwOut{A revision-scoped artifact state and durable identities for subsequent stages}
  Derive a durable job identity from \(p\), \(r\), the stage, and \(\theta\)\;
  \If{that job has succeeded}{\Return its published artifact state\;}
  Download and validate the revision under bounded size and time limits\;
  Publish the verified PDF atomically, then parse it into a versioned sidecar\;
  Record the parser identity, quality tier, checksum, and revision lineage\;
  Persist revision-scoped summary and chunk jobs before dispatching them\;
  \If{a controlled failure occurs}{
    Persist a stable failure state and retain any earlier valid artifact\;
  }
  \Return the current artifact state and child-job identities\;
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:document-preparation} states the stage-level invariant independently of a particular worker or storage implementation. Durable identity is established before execution, and downstream work is recorded before delivery to a replaceable queue. This ordering permits retry after a process or queue failure without publishing partial bytes or losing the lineage between a parsed artifact and the exact revision from which it was produced.

Scientific PDFs still vary in embedded text, column structure, marginal furniture, and heading typography. CERMINE describes the reconstruction of document structure from page layout and text cues when PDF files do not encode a reliable semantic hierarchy~\cite{tkaczyk2015cermine}. S2ORC demonstrates the downstream value of retaining paragraphs, section headers, citation mentions, and resolved bibliography links in machine-readable scholarly text~\cite{lo2020s2orc}. These systems motivate Mneme's explicit treatment of structure and provenance; Mneme's parser is evaluated according to its own implementation and evidence tiers. It combines PyMuPDF text blocks with pdfplumber layout information, reconstructs reading order for common two-column pages, filters likely marginal text blocks, and detects section boundaries from layout and heading cues. The \texttt{structured} tier indicates useful text with credible sections; \texttt{text\_only} indicates useful text without sufficient structural confidence; \texttt{abstract\_only} substitutes the provider abstract when the PDF cannot support full-text processing. This hierarchy is a robustness strategy and assigns lower evidence fidelity to degraded tiers.

Chunk construction consumes the ordered sections and splits oversized content near sentence boundaries while retaining controlled overlap, section identity, page range, content hash, token estimate, and revision identity. The selected configuration uses an estimated 450-token upper bound with a 60-token overlap, providing a fixed and reproducible basis for evaluation. Prior work shows that chunk size and context-preserving chunking strategies affect retrieval quality and answer faithfulness~\cite{wang2024bestpractices}. The model label stored with each embedding prevents retrieval and recommendation from comparing vectors produced in incompatible spaces.

Paper-specific Q\&A adds a second evidence boundary, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:qa-flow}. The question is embedded and compared by cosine distance only with chunks from the selected paper revision and embedding model. The repository performs an exact ordered pgvector search. At most two early embedded chunks can be appended as overview anchors without displacing the highest-ranked question-specific evidence. A deterministic reranker combines normalized vector similarity \(D(q,c)\) with content-word overlap \(L(q,c)\):
\begin{equation}
  R(q,c)=0.7D(q,c)+0.3L(q,c).
  \label{eq:qa-rerank}
\end{equation}

Equation~\ref{eq:qa-rerank} keeps the second-stage score inspectable: semantic proximity remains dominant, and lexical overlap supplies a task-specific correction without adding another learned model.

If no evidence exists or the best reranked score is below the configured threshold, the service returns a fixed refusal without invoking a language model. Otherwise, the generator receives numbered excerpts with section and page context and must return source markers drawn from those excerpts. The post-generation mechanism discards out-of-range markers and assesses local content-word overlap between a citation-bearing claim and its cited chunk. RAGTruth shows that retrieved context can coexist with unsupported or contradictory generation~\cite{niu2024ragtruth}, while ALCE evaluates answer correctness separately from citation quality and divides citation quality into support coverage and citation relevance~\cite{gao2023alce}. Mneme therefore uses \emph{matched}, \emph{partial}, and \emph{insufficient evidence} to report revision membership, marker validity, and local lexical support. This source-matching mechanism does not evaluate semantic entailment or broader factual accuracy.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
  \caption{Revision-scoped answer generation and source checking}
  \label{alg:qa-evidence}
  \KwIn{Paper \(p\), revision \(r\), question \(q\), embedding model \(m\), retrieval count \(k\), evidence threshold \(\tau\)}
  \KwOut{Answer text, valid source records, and a source-match state}
  Embed \(q\) and retrieve \(k\) candidates filtered by \(p\), \(r\), and \(m\)\;
  Add at most two non-duplicate overview anchors from revision \(r\)\;
  Compute \(R(q,c)=0.7D(q,c)+0.3L(q,c)\) and order the evidence\;
  \If{the evidence is empty or its best score is below \(\tau\)}{\Return the fixed insufficient-evidence response\;}
  Generate an answer from the selected, numbered excerpts\;
  \If{the answer is empty or contains no usable marker}{\Return the fixed insufficient-evidence response\;}
  Discard invalid markers and measure local support for the remaining citations\;
  Assign \emph{matched}, \emph{partial}, or \emph{insufficient} from the valid-marker and overlap checks\;
  \Return the answer, valid source records, and assigned state\;
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:qa-evidence} makes each reported source-match state reproducible. The retrieval filter establishes revision membership, and the final checks establish marker validity and local lexical support. The returned state records the outcome of these checks for the client and for later evaluation.

Model-facing stages communicate through provider-independent request and result objects. Routing selects a configured model by task; a canonical cache key includes task, provider, model, prompt version, messages, and output limit; and a Redis budget guard prevents new calls after the configured daily counter reaches its threshold. The counter is an operational stop-loss, and concurrent in-flight requests can still cross the threshold. Persisted summary artifacts retain provider, model, prompt version, input identity, generation parameters, token counts, latency, and estimated cost. Persisted Q\&A messages retain the answer, citations, source-match state, provider, model, prompt version, and optional usage, latency, and cost telemetry. They omit the complete generation request, so the records support audit and partial reconstruction without guaranteeing bit-for-bit regeneration. Secrets and raw behavioral history are not included in model prompts.

\subsection{Interest Memory, Recommendation, and Citation Graph}

Implicit-feedback systems must distinguish an observed action from the confidence with which that action represents preference. Hu, Koren, and Volinsky formulate this distinction explicitly for implicit data~\cite{hu2008implicit}, while temporal recommendation models show that user preference and item effects can change over time~\cite{koren2009temporal}. These concerns motivate Mneme's transparent content-space model, whose predictive behavior is evaluated independently in Chapter~4.

The interest model uses auditable deterministic aggregation. Every event contains a client-generated UUID, event type, timezone-aware occurrence time, optional paper identity, and duration only for a paper-open event. Event insertion and profile recomputation occur in one transaction while the user row is locked. A repeated UUID is acknowledged as a duplicate, so an ambiguous mobile retry cannot apply the same observation twice. Raw events remain the authoritative input, while the derived state records its embedding-model identity, behavior-model identity, parameters, confidence, and evidence summary. Replaying the same event history against the same paper embeddings and reference time therefore reconstructs the same state without rewriting explicit preferences.

The model first distinguishes exposure from preference evidence. An impression records that a paper was shown but contributes no positive weight. Opens, saves, shares, and paper-scoped questions provide positive evidence; a skip provides negative evidence only if an impression or open for the same paper occurred during the preceding seven days. This gate prevents an unobserved paper from being interpreted as a rejected topic. Briefing dismissal remains outside the paper-interest profile because it has no paper identity. Table~\ref{tab:behavior-weights} defines these fixed semantics. The magnitudes encode design priorities within this model, not measured certainty about the user's intent.

For eligible event \(i\), let \(b_i\) denote its signed base weight, \(m_i\) its duration multiplier, and \(\Delta_i\) its age in days. Only an open uses duration. If its observed duration is \(d_i\) seconds, clamped to the interval \([0,300]\), the continuous multiplier and dual-timescale temporal decay are
\begin{equation}
  m_i=0.5+\frac{\log(1+d_i/30)}{\log 11},
  \qquad
  g(\Delta_i)=0.70\,2^{-\Delta_i/14}+0.30\,2^{-\Delta_i/60}.
  \label{eq:behavior-temporal-weight}
\end{equation}
A missing duration gives \(m_i=1\), and a non-open event also uses unit duration multiplier. The effective signed evidence is \(\widetilde{w}_i=b_i m_i g(\Delta_i)\); observations older than 180 days contribute no profile evidence. The logarithmic transform lets reading time refine an open without allowing passive dwell time to outweigh an explicit save or share. Combining a 14-day and a 60-day half-life preserves sensitivity to recent changes while retaining a smaller persistent component. These fixed, replayable parameters define the configuration evaluated in this study.

A direct signed average has two undesirable properties: repeated actions on one paper can dominate through frequency, and positive and negative vectors can cancel into a near-zero representation that hides conflicting evidence. Mneme addresses the first property by accumulating positive and negative mass separately for each paper \(j\), then applying diminishing returns:
\begin{equation}
  M_j^+=\sum_{i\in j}\max(\widetilde{w}_i,0),
  \quad M_j^-=\sum_{i\in j}\max(-\widetilde{w}_i,0),
  \quad a_j^+=1-e^{-M_j^+/3},
  \quad a_j^-=1-e^{-M_j^-}.
  \label{eq:behavior-saturation}
\end{equation}
The resulting supports \(a_j^+\) and \(a_j^-\) are bounded below one, so another interaction with an already well-supported paper has less influence than evidence from a distinct paper. Let \(v_j\) be the compatible mean chunk embedding of paper \(j\). Positive and negative interest states are then maintained as independently normalized support-weighted centroids:
\begin{equation}
  u^+=\operatorname{norm}\!\left(\sum_j a_j^+v_j\right),
  \qquad
  u^-=\operatorname{norm}\!\left(\sum_j a_j^-v_j\right).
  \label{eq:behavior-contrastive-profile}
\end{equation}
Here, \(\operatorname{norm}(x)=x/\lVert x\rVert_2\) for a non-degenerate vector; a channel with no compatible evidence is absent. Separate centroids preserve attraction and avoidance when the observations conflict.

The profile also estimates how much influence its behavioral evidence should receive. Let \(M=\sum_j(a_j^++a_j^-)\) be total embedded support, \(D\) the number of distinct embedded papers with non-zero support, and \(Q\) the fraction of eligible evidence papers for which a compatible embedding is available. The bounded confidence is
\begin{equation}
  C=Q\sqrt{\left(1-e^{-M/3}\right)\left(1-e^{-D/3}\right)},
  \qquad 0\leq C\leq 1.
  \label{eq:behavior-confidence}
\end{equation}
This construction requires both evidence mass and diversity, while incomplete embedding coverage reduces confidence directly. Repeated engagement with one paper can strengthen its support but cannot immediately grant the behavioral component its full nominal weight. Missing embeddings do not delete raw observations; they remain available for a later deterministic replay.

Recommendation keeps explicit preference and inferred behavior as separate components. For candidate paper \(p\) with embedding \(v_p\), the contrastive affinity compares alignment with the positive and negative centroids:
\begin{equation}
  A(p)=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(0.5+0.5\left[\cos(u^+,v_p)-\cos(u^-,v_p)\right]\right).
  \label{eq:behavior-affinity}
\end{equation}
An absent channel contributes zero cosine similarity. If \(T(p)\) is explicit-topic match and \(F(p)=2^{-d/7}\) is recency for publication age \(d\), define component values \((x_T,x_B,x_F)=(T(p),A(p),F(p))\) and nominal weights \((\alpha_T,\alpha_B,\alpha_F)=(0.45,0.35C,0.20)\). For the set \(\mathcal{K}(p)\) of available components, the implemented score is
\begin{equation}
  S(p)=\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(p)}\alpha_k x_k}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(p)}\alpha_k}.
  \label{eq:recommendation-score}
\end{equation}
A missing topic or behavioral component is omitted from \(\mathcal{K}(p)\). A zero-confidence profile therefore follows the cold-start signals and gives no behavioral weight to one weak observation. Stable paper UUID ordering resolves exact score ties. Recommendation reasons are selected from components that participated in the score and are not generated after ranking. Work on scrutable recommendation profiles motivates exposing and correcting the representation behind a recommendation~\cite{ramos2024scrutable}; Mneme implements the narrower property that explicit topics remain editable and displayed reasons remain faithful to the deterministic components.

Explicit correction follows a separate state transition from behavioral aggregation. The mobile interest view maintains an editable draft, rejects blank or duplicate topics, and submits the complete normalized topic list. The preference service replaces the explicit list while preserving followed authors and records the update time. A recommended-digest request accepts a previous snapshot only when it is newer than the current preference state; after a correction, that condition fails and the service evaluates the candidate pool with the updated topics. The client displays success only after the updated preference and the newly ranked briefing have both returned. The earlier briefing is retained with its original scores and reasons because it records what was recommended under the preceding state. This sequence makes a correction visible in the next decision without rewriting historical evidence.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
  \caption{Replayable contrastive preference update and candidate ranking}
  \label{alg:behavior-ranking}
  \KwIn{Deduplicated event history \(E\), compatible paper embeddings \(V\), reference time \(t\), candidate set \(P\)}
  \KwOut{Ranked candidates and a versioned evidence summary}
  \ForEach{event \(e_i\in E\)}{
    Exclude stale events and events without a usable paper identity\;
    Admit a negative skip only when recent exposure exists for the same paper\;
    Accumulate signed, time-decayed evidence \(\widetilde{w}_i=b_i m_i g(\Delta_i)\) by paper\;
  }
  Apply the saturation functions in Equation~\ref{eq:behavior-saturation} per paper\;
  Construct \(u^+\) and \(u^-\) from compatible embeddings and compute confidence \(C\)\;
  \ForEach{candidate \(p\in P\)}{
    Compute \(S(p)\) from the available topic, behavioral, and recency components\;
    Derive a reason only from components that participated in \(S(p)\)\;
  }
  \Return candidates ordered by score and stable paper identity, with confidence and evidence counts\;
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:behavior-ranking} separates the replayable computation from its interpretation. The event history and compatible embeddings determine the result for a fixed reference time. The output carries evidence counts and confidence so that a later experiment can inspect why behavioral influence increased, decreased, or remained absent. This procedure supports mechanism evaluation in Chapter~4 without treating interaction weights as direct measurements of human preference.

Figure~\ref{fig:behavior-loop} connects these algebraic choices to the mobile observation boundary and the immutable briefing. The proposed model is a hand-designed content-space representation whose transparency and replayability support controlled mechanism tests. Chapter~4 evaluates its software invariants and ranking behavior; parameter selection and long-term user benefit remain subjects for longitudinal user evidence.

Citation relations use a separate directed graph model. An edge from \(p_s\) to \(p_t\) means that source paper \(p_s\) cites target paper \(p_t\). A provider observation may initially identify one endpoint only by its Semantic Scholar identifier, but at least one endpoint must already be local. Persisting that unresolved observation preserves information that can be repaired when the external identity is subsequently associated with a local paper. Self-edges and duplicates are removed during resolution, and only edges whose endpoints resolve to local paper UUIDs enter the public graph.

The public graph query performs a bidirectional traversal from the selected paper within explicit depth and node limits. Depth is restricted to one or two hops, and the node limit cannot exceed 200. Citation-derived weighting, deterministic label propagation, and weighted-degree ranking enrich the resulting snapshot. Label propagation originates as a local community-detection method in which nodes repeatedly adopt labels supported by their neighbours~\cite{raghavan2007label}. Mneme uses weighted votes, deterministic tie-breaking, and a maximum of ten update rounds within the selected citation neighborhood. The public graph contains paper nodes and directed citation edges. If enrichment fails, chronological citation ordering provides a deterministic fallback and the response marks the algorithm status. Raw citation observations remain persistent, while the enriched view is computed per request. This avoids invalidation rules for stored layouts at the cost of repeated computation over the selected neighborhood.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
  \caption{Bounded citation-graph view construction}
  \label{alg:citation-graph}
  \KwIn{Centre paper \(p_0\), resolved local citation snapshot \(G=(V,E)\), depth \(d\), node limit \(L\)}
  \KwOut{A bounded node-and-edge view with algorithm status}
  Clamp \(d\) and \(L\), then assign each edge a hub-damped citation weight\;
  Expand bidirectionally from \(p_0\) by cumulative path strength until depth or node limit is reached\;
  Retain edges whose endpoints both occur in the selected node set\;
  Initialize every selected node with a unique community label\;
  \For{at most ten rounds}{
    Update each label from the strongest weighted neighbouring label\;
    Resolve ties deterministically and stop when no label changes\;
  }
  Rank nodes by normalized weighted degree while preserving \(p_0\) as the centre\;
  \If{enrichment fails}{Construct a bounded chronological view and set status to \emph{fallback}\;}
  \Return selected papers, directed edges, community labels, ranks, and status\;
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:citation-graph} defines the server-side view and leaves spatial layout to the client. The hub-damped edge weight reduces the influence of highly cited targets during expansion, while the explicit depth and node limits bound response size. Deterministic tie-breaking gives the same logical snapshot the same ordering and grouping. The fallback preserves graph access when enrichment fails and identifies that outcome in the response.

Across document, behavior, and graph paths, the same reliability rule applies: durable domain state precedes replaceable execution or presentation state. Stable identifiers make retries safe; model, parser, behavior, and graph versions prevent silent semantic changes; and public responses expose typed status without returning internal exception text. Paper excerpts sent to model providers exclude raw behavioral history and unnecessary profile fields. Secrets remain outside source and mobile resources, while generated-artifact metadata records enough configuration to interpret a result. Chapter~4 reports implementation and testing evidence for these mechanisms.

\subsection{Mobile State, Recovery, and Event Delivery}
\label{subsec:mobile-state-recovery}

The mobile client preserves the same paper identity while remote work, local storage, and navigation progress at different rates.
When a paper is opened, the client first attempts to load its remote representation.
A validated response is stored before it becomes the visible live state.
If the request fails, a locally stored paper can remain readable with a cached label; question answering and citation-graph requests instead show a retryable error because no local substitute has the same meaning.
An HTTP~202 response is treated as processing rather than failure, and the client polls the durable job until the representation is ready or a safe error is returned.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
  \caption{Origin-aware mobile paper resolution}
  \label{alg:mobile-paper-resolution}
  \KwIn{Selected paper identity \(p\), optional locally stored paper record}
  \KwOut{Typed presentation state with visible content origin}
  Cancel an earlier load, enter \emph{loading} for \(p\), and request its remote representation\;
  \If{the remote request fails}{\Return cached content when available; otherwise enter a retryable error state\;}
  Store a validated remote paper locally before presenting its derived content\;
  \While{the summary response identifies a queued or running job}{
    Display the preparation stage and poll the durable job after a bounded interval\;
    Propagate cancellation without converting it to a visible failure\;
  }
  \eIf{the representation is ready}{Render live content\;}{Enter a retryable error state with the safe public message\;}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:mobile-paper-resolution} gives one paper route a single active load, preventing a late response for a previous route from replacing the newly selected paper.
It also keeps transport failure, server-side processing, cache recovery, and completed content as separate states that the interface can explain.

Graph presentation uses a locally packaged WebView because the force-directed canvas and its interaction bridge are more tractable in Web technology than in a custom Compose drawing stack.
This separation keeps the graph data and task, visual encoding, interaction, and layout mechanism as distinct design layers~\cite{munzner2009nested}.
Android validates unique node identities, the presence of the centre node, and the absence of dangling edges before serializing the graph for the WebView.
The return bridge accepts only an identifier present in the displayed graph and posts the selected paper UUID to the Android main thread.
Native state then retains the selection, relationship context, and \emph{Open Paper} action across navigation.
This boundary leaves layout computation in the WebView while keeping navigation and accessibility semantics in Android.

Behavioral events follow a similarly bounded delivery path.
Impressions, paper opens, and paper-scoped questions carry stable paper identities and client-generated event UUIDs.
They remain in the Room-backed queue until the server acknowledges them, and WorkManager retries delivery after uncertain connectivity or process interruption.
The server reports accepted and duplicate identifiers separately, so replay does not require the client to guess whether the first request arrived.
Raw reading history is not included in language-model prompts, and explicit topics remain editable when the user wants to correct the resulting recommendation state.

\section{API Design}
\label{sec:api-design}

The API is the boundary at which the mobile experience, durable domain model, and background engine must agree on meaning. Mneme uses a resource-oriented JSON contract under \texttt{/v1}, with \texttt{snake\_case} fields, typed UUID identities, and ISO~8601 timestamps. FastAPI generates the runtime OpenAPI~3.1.0 schema, while a frozen OpenAPI~3.0.3 document serves as the compatibility specification against which the public surface is checked. The interface contains 13 public operations and keeps internal worker stages behind the service boundary. Papers, preferences, briefings, questions, events, graph neighborhoods, and jobs each have a public representation that can be generated from the application and checked against that compatibility specification. This contract-first arrangement lets the Android client map network objects into its own domain models without depending on database tables or model-provider payloads.

Table~\ref{tab:api-surface} groups the operations by responsibility. The unauthenticated health operation establishes only that the application process can answer an HTTP request; it does not certify PostgreSQL, Redis, or external-provider availability. Protected operations use a pre-provisioned principal so that user-scoped resources retain explicit ownership even in a single-user demonstration. Collection endpoints use opaque keyset cursors and a default page size of 20, bounded between 1 and 50. The client consequently cannot derive ordering assumptions from the cursor representation, and insertion of a newer paper or briefing does not make an offset silently refer to a different record.

The most consequential interface decision is to represent long work as a state transition with a durable job resource. A summary request first checks the selected revision for a stored artifact. A ready artifact produces HTTP~200. Otherwise, the service creates or reuses the earliest missing revision-scoped job, persists its durable identity, attempts dispatch, and returns HTTP~202 with that job. The Android client can then poll \texttt{/jobs/\{job\_id\}} and repeat the original read after success. The deterministic recommendation path can return a briefing synchronously, while its schema admits the same job representation for a slower scoring path. This separation keeps transport timeout, queue acceptance, model execution, and completed domain state from collapsing into one ambiguous result.

Figure~\ref{fig:api-sequence} illustrates the corresponding state choreography. The database is authoritative for both the artifact and the job; ARQ carries a replaceable attempt. A queue failure after durable job persistence is reported as a temporary service error and releases the dispatch claim, so a later request can dispatch the same durable job without creating a second logical operation. The public job never contains a stack trace, prompt, token, or provider exception. After the worker publishes its artifact and updates the job state, a poll observes success and the resource read returns the completed representation.

Idempotency is expressed differently for the two write patterns. Pipeline creation derives a uniqueness key from canonical stage inputs, so repeated summary requests converge on one logical job. Behavioral ingestion accepts client-generated event UUIDs and reports how many items were newly accepted and how many were duplicates. The entire event batch and behavior recomputation execute transactionally for one user. Recalculation derives the positive and negative profile embeddings, bounded confidence, and evidence summary from the authoritative event history without exposing those persistence fields through the ingestion response. If any referenced paper or timestamp violates the contract, the whole batch is rejected. These semantics let the mobile client retain an event until acknowledgement and retry after uncertain connectivity without needing to infer whether the first request reached the server.

The question endpoint is synchronous because its useful response is the answer and its source-match state. A request identifies one paper, an optional conversation, and a question of at most 500 characters. The conversation UUID preserves ownership and paper scope, while generation remains grounded in the new question and selected revision. The response presents source records at section and chunk granularity. The graph endpoint likewise presents paper nodes and directed citation edges. Both interfaces keep provider observations, prompt context, and other persistence details behind the service boundary.

Every failure crosses the API through one envelope containing \texttt{code}, \texttt{message}, \texttt{request\_id}, and optional structured \texttt{details}. Validation details describe locations and error types without echoing rejected values. Database and unexpected failures are logged with the request identity but become non-sensitive service or internal errors at the boundary. The same sanitized \texttt{X-Request-ID} is attached to the response, giving the client a support handle without exposing stack traces. Stable application codes distinguish, for example, an unknown paper, an unavailable queue, invalid pagination, inadequate configuration, and malformed input even when several cases share an HTTP status.

All operations except process health require an opaque Bearer token representing the pre-provisioned user. The raw token is supplied from deployment and mobile configuration and is absent from source resources; the server stores its SHA-256 digest and compares the received token in constant time. This mechanism supplies stable ownership for the single-user study deployment. A production multi-user deployment would extend the same boundary with account lifecycle, authorization, transport, and secret-management controls.

Three alternatives clarify the contract's priorities. Blocking until document processing finishes would produce a simpler response type but would couple mobile timeouts to PDF and model latency; the explicit job resource was preferred. Offset pagination would be easier to inspect manually but is unstable under insertion; opaque keyset cursors were preferred. Returning database or provider objects directly would reduce mapping code but would leak storage evolution and sensitive diagnostics into the app; explicit Pydantic response schemas and a common error envelope were preferred. The added translation code is therefore a deliberate cost paid for compatibility, failure clarity, and a narrower privacy surface.

The onboarding path follows progressive disclosure. The first screen explains the product in terms of reading outcomes and asks for one arXiv reference. The seed category initializes an explicit preference and supplies the context from which the backend prepares the first briefing. After entry, the interest view exposes that profile so that topics can be added, edited, or deleted before a later briefing is requested. This creates a populated first session with a short setup sequence while preserving a direct correction path. It also follows the human--AI interaction principle that a system should communicate what it can do and make correction possible when an inferred result is wrong~\cite{amershi2019guidelines}.

The briefing screen supports rapid comparison. Each card presents title, authorship, category or date context, a short description, a normalized relevance score, and a concise reason before the primary open action. Recommendation rationale is positioned near the score because a detached explanation is unlikely to be used during triage. Saving and preference tuning remain secondary actions so they do not compete with opening the paper. An empty briefing preserves the navigation and offers search or interest correction without fabricating recommendations.

Paper detail changes the information density. The title and source identity remain at the top, while the generated account separates overview, claims, method, and limitations. Citation chips and source-match labels sit adjacent to the text they qualify. Research on position-sensitive reading assistance shows the value of presenting contextual definitions at the point where a reader encounters an unfamiliar term or symbol~\cite{head2021scholarphi}. Mneme applies the same locality principle to evidence access: the source action is placed beside the generated material it qualifies. Each source record exposes section and chunk context and opens the canonical paper source. This placement supports verification, while the labels identify the result of local source matching. A partial or abstract-only representation uses text as well as colour to disclose its evidence tier.

The Q\&A screen inherits the selected paper identity and asks a question within that scope. The answer body and source list are presented as one inspection unit, with source markers visible beside the generated response. A matched, partial, or insufficient-evidence label describes the post-generation check defined in Section~\ref{sec:engine-architecture}. Conversation identity groups related exchanges, while every new answer remains grounded in the selected revision and current question. When no adequate excerpt is found, the insufficient-evidence response occupies the normal answer region and remains distinct from a transport failure.

Controlled fixture content kept the screen hierarchy and task affordances consistent across the five moderated sessions. Runtime behavior and model quality are evaluated separately in Chapter~4.

The graph requires stronger affordance than a conventional list because spatial proximity does not tell a first-time user what is actionable. Interaction taxonomies for information visualization identify selection and exploration as distinct operations that the interface must support~\cite{yi2007interaction}; task-oriented visualization design likewise asks why the user acts, what data is targeted, and how the operation is performed~\cite{brehmer2013typology}. Mneme's task is to identify a related paper, inspect its relationship to the centre, and continue to the paper record. A short instruction appears above the canvas. Selection produces a persistent visual state, an explanation of the relationship, and a visible \emph{Open Paper} action. A legend explains edge direction and algorithmic grouping. Visually truncated node labels retain their complete title in the native preview. Android stores the selected identity, allowing the graph and native preview to restore the same selection after the reader returns from paper detail. The implemented public graph contains paper nodes and citation edges. The visual system uses a restrained navy, teal, gold, green, and red palette consistent with the diagrams in this chapter. Operational meaning is carried by text labels, icons, selected outlines, and action placement as well as colour. Large targets, scalable text, semantic content descriptions, predictable back navigation, and a native selected-node summary define the accessibility requirements for touch and screen-reader use. The formative task study below evaluates discoverability of the principal flow. Evaluation with assistive technologies and a wider range of users would extend this evidence to accessibility in use.

\section{Usability Testing}
\label{sec:usability-testing}

The architecture and interaction design establish what the system should expose. A formative usability study examined whether first-time readers could discover the intended path in an interactive prototype. Five participants were tested individually on comprehension and navigation across the central research loop. Task completion counts and qualitative observations from the moderated sessions show whether participants could locate and act on a recommendation explanation, source, answer, graph node, or preference control. Fixed prototype content held these interaction targets constant; system and model quality are evaluated through the experiments in Chapter~4.

\subsection{Usability Testing Script}
\label{subsec:usability-script}

Each session began with the same explanation: the study evaluated the interface; confusing behavior was useful evidence; participation could stop at any time; and the participant should think aloud. Concurrent think-aloud protocols can reveal usability problems, although the form of verbalization can influence the problems observed~\cite{fan2019thinkaloud}. The script therefore kept the instruction and facilitator behavior consistent across sessions. A test identity was already available so that routine setup would not consume the session. The facilitator presented tasks without naming a screen or control and responded to requests for help with non-directive prompts. This procedure preserved false starts and hesitation, particularly for controls whose appearance suggested information without making the available action clear.

The script's research questions matched the story-map transitions. It asked whether a participant could understand why a briefing item was recommended, recover a paper's main contribution and one source, ask a paper-scoped question and inspect the cited evidence, move from a paper into related work through the graph, and correct the interest profile. The pre-session questions covered paper-reading frequency, research area, discovery practice, prior AI-reading tools, and familiarity with citation graphs. The post-session questions asked which component was useful or confusing, whether reasons and citations affected confidence, and what should change. These questions provided context for interpreting the observed behavior. The retained study record supports task-level completion counts and qualitative observations, which form the basis of the analysis below.

Table~\ref{tab:usability-script} defines the five tasks and the observable success criterion used in analysis. Target time and action counts were specified before testing as facilitation guides. The retained results contain success counts rather than participant-level timing, tap, or hesitation records, so task completion is the primary quantitative outcome. T3 measures whether the participant located both an answer and its cited excerpt; the accompanying observations describe how participants approached that verification step.

\subsection{Usability Tests and Results}
\label{subsec:usability-results}

Figure~\ref{fig:usability-results} reports the recorded completion counts. All five participants completed briefing triage (T1), source inspection (T2), and cited Q\&A inspection (T3). Two of five completed the graph transition (T4), and four of five saved a preference change (T5). Across the five tasks, 21 of 25 participant--task attempts met the corresponding criterion, a descriptive proportion of 84\%. The 15 of 15 completions across T1--T3 show that, under explicit task prompts, participants could move from a recommendation reason to paper detail, locate a source-oriented summary path, and inspect evidence associated with a Q\&A response. Session notes described the briefing as readily legible and citation-backed answers as useful. They also indicated that verification deserved a stronger visual cue. A reader responding to an explicit verification task may locate a source control that is less noticeable during ordinary reading. The design therefore retains the briefing structure and places source labels and citation chips closer to the claims they qualify. Chapter~4 complements this interaction result with separate measures of system and answer behavior.

Two participants completed T4 and used the graph to open a related paper. The other three did not infer which elements could be selected or how selection led to a paper, although the visual form attracted attention. This concentrated result motivated three coupled changes: an instruction cue states that a node can be tapped, the selected node acquires a persistent state with relationship context, and an \emph{Open Paper} action remains visible after selection. These changes directly address the observed discoverability problem, and software tests verify the revised interaction behavior.

T5 was completed by four participants. The single failure and accompanying observation indicated that participants could understand the interest controls once they found them, while their initial placement lacked prominence. Preference tuning therefore remains one action away from the briefing and receives an explicit settings entry. This placement also supports the broader transparency objective: a user who sees an irrelevant reason can inspect and correct explicit interests without leaving the reading loop. Chapter~4 examines the effect of an explicit profile correction on subsequent ranking under controlled conditions.

The study established a legible reading spine for briefing triage, source inspection, cited Q\&A, and interest correction. It also isolated graph discoverability as the principal interaction problem: three participants did not complete T4, and their observed difficulty led directly to the instruction cue, persistent selection state, relationship preview, and visible \emph{Open Paper} action. The five moderated sessions and controlled prototype content support this formative design conclusion. A repeated study with the revised graph, participant background measures, and per-task timing would quantify the effect of those changes and extend the present findings beyond prompted first-use interaction.
