The design of Mneme begins with a continuous research activity rather than with a catalogue of screens. A researcher repeatedly encounters new literature, decides what deserves attention, reconstructs what has already been read, checks evidence, and revises an evolving account of personal interests. Chapter~2 establishes the customer evidence behind this activity. The present chapter translates that evidence into the application's story map, acceptance boundaries, engine architecture, interface contract, and interaction design. It also reports the formative usability study that tested whether the principal workflow was legible in an interactive prototype.

The chapter treats traceability as a cross-cutting design property. Paper-derived artifacts are associated with an observed source revision; generated answers are constrained to retrieved excerpts from one paper; behavioral adaptation separates positive and negative evidence and calibrates its influence by evidence confidence; and mobile states distinguish live, cached, controlled, degraded, and failed outcomes. These choices do not make every model output correct. They make the origin and limitations of an output available to the surrounding software and, where useful, to the reader. This distinction connects the representation, evidence, interest, and interaction challenges introduced in Chapter~1 without replacing the supervisor-defined structure of this chapter.

\section{Storymap and Features}
\label{sec:storymap-features}

The customer research revealed two complementary requirements. First, participants needed to reduce the cost of triage: abstracts, figures, venues, authors, and code availability were used as rapid signals before a full reading decision. Second, they needed to recover and verify information after the initial encounter. The application therefore cannot be reduced to either a search engine or a summarizer. Its workflow must connect discovery, interpretation, evidence inspection, retention, and renewed discovery. The story map in Figure~\ref{fig:storymap} expresses this dependency as five user-oriented stages.

The first stage, receiving new papers, is deliberately an engine-facing stage because every later interaction depends on the quality of the source representation. Mneme observes metadata from scholarly providers, associates each observation with a logical work and a specific revision, acquires the corresponding PDF where permitted, and produces a structured or explicitly degraded representation. A paper that cannot be parsed confidently remains available through its metadata and abstract rather than disappearing from the user's information stream. This fallback preserves continuity while preventing an abstract-only representation from being mistaken for full-text evidence.

Learning interests combines direct instruction with observed interaction. A first-use flow asks for research topics and a seed paper, which gives the system a useful starting point without requiring a long preference form. The mobile client records paper impressions as exposure evidence and records opens and paper-scoped questions as supported positive observations; each event can be uploaded with duplicate-safe identity. The shared event contract further assigns semantics to explicit save, share, and exposed-skip actions without interpreting the absence of an action as preference evidence. The design keeps explicit topics and the contrastive behavioral profile distinguishable because a chosen topic and an inferred signal have different epistemic meanings. The user interface can consequently offer recommendation reasons and correction controls without presenting behavioral inference as certain intent.

Planning what to read turns the candidate stream into a research briefing. The default product rhythm is weekly, while an explicit refresh can request an on-demand recommendation snapshot. Each entry contains the paper identity, a concise description, a bounded relevance score, and a reason derived from the scoring components. The briefing is an immutable snapshot: later preference changes affect a later briefing rather than rewriting the rationale for an earlier recommendation. This property supports reproducibility and also gives the mobile cache a stable object to restore.

Asking with sources contains two related but non-identical experiences. A structured summary supports rapid triage by separating the paper's short account, claims, method, and limitations. Paper-specific question answering addresses a narrower information need by retrieving excerpts from one observed revision and asking a language model to answer from that evidence. The interface exposes source labels when an answer is returned and exposes an insufficient-evidence state when the retrieval boundary cannot support a response. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) generally combines parametric generation with an explicit non-parametric memory~\cite{lewis2020rag}; Mneme narrows that memory to the selected paper revision so that the evidence set remains inspectable.

Exploring connections uses a bounded directed citation graph. The central paper, locally known citing papers, and locally known referenced papers form a neighborhood that can be traversed without loading an unbounded scholarly network onto a phone. The graph is not presented as complete: unresolved external identities remain outside the public view, and the response records whether algorithmic enrichment or a deterministic citation-only fallback produced the ordering. Selection leads to a native preview and a persistent action that returns the reader to a paper detail screen, keeping graph exploration subordinate to the reading task rather than making visualization an isolated destination.

The five stages form a closed loop rather than a linear onboarding funnel. Receiving and preparation make evidence available; explicit and observed interests make ranking possible; briefing interactions identify the next paper; source-grounded reading creates further observations; and graph traversal discovers additional works that may re-enter the preparation pipeline. The architecture must therefore preserve identity and failure information across repeated cycles. That requirement explains why the following acceptance criteria include provenance, idempotency, boundedness, and degraded operation in addition to visible screen outcomes.

Several criteria intentionally specify a visible limitation. An abstract-only representation, an insufficient-evidence answer, a fallback graph, and a cached briefing are useful outcomes only when their origin is not hidden. This principle avoids a common false-positive acceptance pattern in which any non-empty screen is interpreted as successful execution. It also creates a consistent vocabulary between the API and the client: quality, source match, algorithm status, processing state, and content origin are part of the feature contract.

The criteria also separate repeatability from quality. A duplicate-safe event or an idempotent document job can be accepted because its state transition is deterministic, whereas the relevance of a recommended paper and the usefulness of a generated answer require empirical evaluation. Conversely, a negative result does not necessarily violate the contract. A refusal satisfies the Q\&A contract when evidence is inadequate, and a partial paper satisfies the ingestion contract when the source cannot support full-text processing. This separation allows Chapter~4 to report software correctness and empirical quality without conflating them.

\section{Engine Architecture}
\label{sec:engine-architecture}

\subsection{System Boundaries and End-to-End Flow}

Mneme separates the latency-sensitive mobile experience from document processing and model inference. Figure~\ref{fig:system-architecture} shows four operational regions. The Android application owns presentation, navigation, explicitly labelled local state, and durable capture of supported interactions. The FastAPI service owns authentication, validation, public state transitions, and orchestration. PostgreSQL with pgvector owns durable domain and vector state, while Redis and ARQ carry replaceable execution attempts and cache entries. Scholarly sources and model providers remain external dependencies whose responses are validated before they affect durable state.

The Android region follows a unidirectional state flow. A screen emits an action to its ViewModel, the ViewModel obtains a domain result through a repository, and the result becomes a typed UI state observed by Compose. Retrofit and OkHttp map the versioned API into transport objects, while repositories reconcile remote responses with Room snapshots before presentation. This boundary prevents a network response from becoming interface state directly and allows controlled and live repository implementations to exercise the same ViewModel and rendering path. Loading, content, processing, cached, and error conditions remain explicit rather than being inferred from nullable fields or the survival of an earlier screen.

Stable paper identifiers connect the briefing, paper detail, paper-scoped Q\&A, and citation graph without serializing a complete paper object into a navigation route; source records retain the same paper identity when the reader inspects supporting material. DataStore retains small preference and mode values, while Room retains briefing and paper metadata together with a duplicate-safe event queue. A local WebView renders a repository-vendored graph asset, but node identity is mirrored through a restricted JavaScript bridge into a native Compose preview. The graph selection is saved against its centre-paper route, so returning from an opened paper restores the prior graph context. This arrangement preserves the interaction benefits of a force-directed graph while keeping navigation and accessibility-relevant selection state in the native layer.

The server region is intentionally divided between synchronous request handling and asynchronously recoverable work. Catalogue reads, preference updates, event ingestion, recommendation scoring, and bounded graph assembly operate through domain services and repositories. PDF acquisition, parsing, summarization, chunking, embedding, and scheduled briefing assembly use durable jobs and ARQ workers because their latency and external dependencies do not fit a single mobile request. The API can therefore return either a complete representation or an accepted job identity without holding a connection open during document preparation.

PostgreSQL is the source of truth because papers, revisions, citations, preferences, generated artifacts, and job histories must survive queue eviction and process restart. pgvector resides in the same database so that vector retrieval can be filtered by the relational paper and revision identities in one query. Redis remains valuable for low-latency completion caching, budget counters, and ARQ delivery, but queue presence is not interpreted as durable completion. Local file storage holds PDF and parsed sidecar artifacts under UUID-derived paper and revision directories. This is simpler than object storage for the study deployment and retains an explicit replacement boundary if storage scale becomes a research concern.

The external region is treated as untrusted input rather than as an extension of the database. arXiv metadata and PDF URLs must agree on a logical identifier and explicit revision. Semantic Scholar observations can refer to papers outside the local catalogue, so unresolved endpoint identities are retained without being exposed as fabricated local nodes. Model responses are parsed, associated with provider and prompt metadata, and checked against output contracts before persistence. These boundaries allow a provider failure to become a controlled error or degraded artifact instead of corrupting the logical paper history.

The principal architectural alternative was a phone-centred design in which document extraction and language-model inference execute on the device. That approach would reduce server dependence but would impose model, memory, battery, and background-execution constraints unrelated to the main research questions. Mneme instead keeps the mobile client responsible for interaction continuity and makes computational services replaceable behind a stable API. A second alternative was a single synchronous document-processing request. It was rejected because partial failure would force the entire pipeline to restart and would make it difficult to identify which revision and stage produced an artifact. The staged architecture trades additional state for recovery, provenance, and bounded retries.

\subsection{Revision-Aware Document and Evidence Flow}

The data model distinguishes a logical paper \(p\) from an observed revision \(r\). The logical paper retains discovery metadata and stable external identifiers; the revision retains the source checksum, byte size, parser version, parse checksum, quality tier, and timestamps associated with one content state. A derived artifact \(a\) is therefore interpreted as
\begin{equation}
  a = f\!\left(p,r,h,\theta\right),
  \label{eq:artifact-lineage}
\end{equation}
In Equation~\ref{eq:artifact-lineage}, \(h\) is the content checksum and \(\theta\) contains the transformation identity relevant to the artifact, such as parser, embedding model, prompt, or generator configuration. Equality of the logical paper identifier does not imply that two artifacts are interchangeable. Reuse is valid only when the material lineage fields for the artifact class match.

The arXiv synchronization path first normalizes the provider identifier, then parses and validates an entire Atom page before persistence. Metadata for a logical work is refreshed only when the provider exposes a newer source timestamp, while a previously unseen revision creates a distinct version row. Network access occurs outside the database transaction, and the validated page is persisted atomically. A serial request limiter and bounded retry policy for transport, rate-limit, and server errors prevent concurrent background tasks from turning provider pressure into inconsistent local state.

Figure~\ref{fig:document-dag} shows the resulting processing directed acyclic graph. Download, parse, summary, chunk, and embedding stages carry both the logical paper UUID and the exact revision UUID. Parsing is the branch point: a structured document can feed summary generation in parallel with deterministic chunk construction, and chunk construction subsequently feeds embedding. Readiness is reconciled from artifacts for the selected revision rather than from any artifact attached to the logical paper.

Each stage receives an idempotency key computed from canonical JSON containing the stage, pipeline version, scope, and material inputs. Download identity includes the paper and revision; parse identity additionally includes the source checksum and parser version; embedding identity includes the parsed artifact and embedding model. A uniqueness constraint causes concurrent submissions of the same logical work to converge on one durable job. The database records queued, running, succeeded, or failed state and a safe public error code. Redis receives an attempt identifier derived from the durable job and the next dispatch identity, while the worker increments the persisted attempt only when execution begins. A stale dispatch lease can be recovered without changing the durable job UUID.

Document storage applies the same all-or-nothing principle to bytes. The downloader validates the URL, declared and streamed size, PDF signature, and checksum. It writes to a temporary file, synchronizes the bytes, and atomically replaces the destination. Parsed sidecars use the same publication rule and include schema version, paper and revision identifiers, source checksum, page count, ordered sections, page ranges, quality tier, fallback reason, and timestamp. A crash can therefore leave a complete previous artifact or an identifiable temporary file, rather than a partially written artifact that appears valid.

Scientific PDFs still vary in embedded text, column structure, marginal furniture, and heading typography. The parser combines PyMuPDF text blocks with pdfplumber layout information, reconstructs reading order for common two-column pages, filters likely marginal text blocks, and detects section boundaries from layout and heading cues. The \texttt{structured} tier indicates useful text with credible sections; \texttt{text\_only} indicates useful text without sufficient structural confidence; \texttt{abstract\_only} substitutes the provider abstract when the PDF cannot support full-text processing. This hierarchy is a robustness strategy, not a claim that degraded tiers preserve equivalent evidence fidelity.

Chunk construction consumes the ordered sections and splits oversized content near sentence boundaries while retaining bounded overlap, section identity, page range, content hash, token estimate, and revision identity. The selected configuration uses an estimated 450-token upper bound with a 60-token overlap. This is a reproducible design setting rather than an asserted optimum. Prior work shows that chunk size and context-preserving chunking strategies create task-dependent trade-offs among retrieval quality, answer faithfulness, and processing cost~\cite{wang2024bestpractices}. The model label stored with each embedding prevents retrieval and recommendation from comparing vectors produced in incompatible spaces.

Paper-specific Q\&A adds a second evidence boundary, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:qa-flow}. The question is embedded and compared by cosine distance only with chunks from the selected paper revision and embedding model. The repository performs an ordered pgvector search; the schema does not add an approximate-nearest-neighbor index, so the design does not claim approximate search. At most two early embedded chunks can be appended as overview anchors without displacing the highest-ranked question-specific evidence. A deterministic reranker combines normalized vector similarity \(D(q,c)\) with content-word overlap \(L(q,c)\):
\begin{equation}
  R(q,c)=0.7D(q,c)+0.3L(q,c).
  \label{eq:qa-rerank}
\end{equation}

Equation~\ref{eq:qa-rerank} deliberately keeps the second-stage score inspectable: semantic proximity remains dominant, while lexical overlap supplies a bounded task-specific correction rather than an additional learned model.

If no evidence exists or the best reranked score is below the configured threshold, the service returns a fixed refusal without invoking a language model. Otherwise, the generator receives numbered excerpts with section and page context and must return source markers drawn from those excerpts. The post-generation mechanism discards out-of-range markers and assesses local content-word overlap between a citation-bearing claim and its cited chunk. RAGTruth demonstrates that retrieved context can coexist with unsupported or contradictory generation~\cite{niu2024ragtruth}; source membership is therefore not treated as factual correctness. ALCE similarly evaluates answer correctness separately from citation quality and decomposes citation quality into support coverage and citation relevance~\cite{gao2023alce}. Mneme's public labels remain narrower: matched, partial, or insufficient evidence describe this source check, not scientific truth.

Model-facing stages communicate through provider-independent request and result objects. Routing selects a configured model by task; a canonical cache key includes task, provider, model, prompt version, messages, and output limit; and a Redis budget guard prevents new calls after the configured daily counter reaches its threshold. The counter update is an operational stop-loss rather than a transactional spending guarantee because concurrent in-flight requests can cross the threshold. Persisted summary artifacts retain provider, model, prompt version, input identity, generation parameters, token counts, latency, and estimated cost. Persisted Q\&A messages retain the answer, citations, source-match state, provider, model, prompt version, and optional usage, latency, and cost telemetry, but not a complete generation request. These records support audit and partial reconstruction; they do not guarantee bit-for-bit regeneration. Secrets and raw behavioral history are not included in model prompts.

\subsection{Interest Memory, Recommendation, and Citation Graph}

The interest model uses auditable deterministic aggregation rather than a hidden online learner. Every event contains a client-generated UUID, event type, timezone-aware occurrence time, optional paper identity, and duration only for a paper-open event. Event insertion and profile recomputation occur in one transaction while the user row is locked. A repeated UUID is acknowledged as a duplicate, so an ambiguous mobile retry cannot apply the same observation twice. Raw events remain the authoritative input, while the derived state records its embedding-model identity, behavior-model identity, parameters, confidence, and evidence summary. Replaying the same event history against the same paper embeddings and reference time therefore reconstructs the same state without rewriting explicit preferences.

The model first distinguishes exposure from preference evidence. An impression records that a paper was shown but contributes no positive weight. Opens, saves, shares, and paper-scoped questions provide positive evidence; a skip provides negative evidence only if an impression or open for the same paper occurred during the preceding seven days. This gate prevents an unobserved paper from being interpreted as a rejected topic. Briefing dismissal remains outside the paper-interest profile because it has no paper identity. Table~\ref{tab:behavior-weights} defines these fixed semantics. The magnitudes encode design priorities within this model, not measured certainty about the user's intent.

For eligible event \(i\), let \(b_i\) denote its signed base weight, \(m_i\) its duration multiplier, and \(\Delta_i\) its age in days. Only an open uses duration. If its observed duration is \(d_i\) seconds, clamped to the interval \([0,300]\), the continuous multiplier and dual-timescale temporal decay are
\begin{equation}
  m_i=0.5+\frac{\log(1+d_i/30)}{\log 11},
  \qquad
  g(\Delta_i)=0.70\,2^{-\Delta_i/14}+0.30\,2^{-\Delta_i/60}.
  \label{eq:behavior-temporal-weight}
\end{equation}
A missing duration gives \(m_i=1\), and a non-open event also uses unit duration multiplier. The effective signed evidence is \(\widetilde{w}_i=b_i m_i g(\Delta_i)\); observations older than 180 days contribute no profile evidence. The bounded logarithmic transform lets reading time refine an open without allowing passive dwell time to outweigh an explicit save or share. Combining a 14-day and a 60-day half-life preserves sensitivity to recent changes while retaining a smaller persistent component. These values are fixed, replayable parameters rather than claimed optima.

A direct signed average has two undesirable properties: repeated actions on one paper can dominate through frequency, and positive and negative vectors can cancel into a near-zero representation that hides conflicting evidence. Mneme addresses the first property by accumulating positive and negative mass separately for each paper \(j\), then applying diminishing returns:
\begin{equation}
  M_j^+=\sum_{i\in j}\max(\widetilde{w}_i,0),
  \quad M_j^-=\sum_{i\in j}\max(-\widetilde{w}_i,0),
  \quad a_j^+=1-e^{-M_j^+/3},
  \quad a_j^-=1-e^{-M_j^-}.
  \label{eq:behavior-saturation}
\end{equation}
The resulting supports \(a_j^+\) and \(a_j^-\) are bounded below one, so another interaction with an already well-supported paper has less influence than evidence from a distinct paper. Let \(v_j\) be the compatible mean chunk embedding of paper \(j\). Positive and negative interest states are then maintained as independently normalized support-weighted centroids:
\begin{equation}
  u^+=\operatorname{norm}\!\left(\sum_j a_j^+v_j\right),
  \qquad
  u^-=\operatorname{norm}\!\left(\sum_j a_j^-v_j\right).
  \label{eq:behavior-contrastive-profile}
\end{equation}
Here, \(\operatorname{norm}(x)=x/\lVert x\rVert_2\) for a non-degenerate vector; a channel with no compatible evidence is absent. Separate centroids preserve the distinction between attraction and avoidance instead of forcing contradictory observations to erase one another.

The profile also estimates how much influence its behavioral evidence should receive. Let \(M=\sum_j(a_j^++a_j^-)\) be total embedded support, \(D\) the number of distinct embedded papers with non-zero support, and \(Q\) the fraction of eligible evidence papers for which a compatible embedding is available. The bounded confidence is
\begin{equation}
  C=Q\sqrt{\left(1-e^{-M/3}\right)\left(1-e^{-D/3}\right)},
  \qquad 0\leq C\leq 1.
  \label{eq:behavior-confidence}
\end{equation}
This construction requires both evidence mass and diversity, while incomplete embedding coverage reduces confidence directly. Repeated engagement with one paper can strengthen its support but cannot immediately grant the behavioral component its full nominal weight. Missing embeddings do not delete raw observations; they remain available for a later deterministic replay.

Recommendation keeps explicit preference and inferred behavior as separate components. For candidate paper \(p\) with embedding \(v_p\), the contrastive affinity compares alignment with the positive and negative centroids:
\begin{equation}
  A(p)=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(0.5+0.5\left[\cos(u^+,v_p)-\cos(u^-,v_p)\right]\right).
  \label{eq:behavior-affinity}
\end{equation}
An absent channel contributes zero cosine similarity. If \(T(p)\) is explicit-topic match and \(F(p)=2^{-d/7}\) is recency for publication age \(d\), define component values \((x_T,x_B,x_F)=(T(p),A(p),F(p))\) and nominal weights \((\alpha_T,\alpha_B,\alpha_F)=(0.45,0.35C,0.20)\). For the set \(\mathcal{K}(p)\) of available components, the implemented score is
\begin{equation}
  S(p)=\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(p)}\alpha_k x_k}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(p)}\alpha_k}.
  \label{eq:recommendation-score}
\end{equation}
A missing topic or behavioral component is omitted from \(\mathcal{K}(p)\). A zero-confidence profile therefore follows the cold-start signals rather than treating one weak observation as a mature interest model. Stable paper UUID ordering resolves exact score ties. Recommendation reasons are selected from components that participated in the score instead of being generated after ranking. Work on scrutable recommendation profiles motivates exposing and correcting the representation behind a recommendation~\cite{ramos2024scrutable}; Mneme implements the narrower property that explicit topics remain editable and displayed reasons remain faithful to the deterministic components.

Figure~\ref{fig:behavior-loop} connects these algebraic choices to the mobile observation boundary and the immutable briefing. The proposed model remains a hand-designed content-space representation: its transparency and replayability support controlled mechanism tests, but neither the parameter values nor long-term user benefit follow from the equations alone. Chapter~4 therefore evaluates software invariants and controlled ranking behavior separately from claims that would require longitudinal user evidence.

Citation relations use a separate directed graph model. An edge from \(p_s\) to \(p_t\) means that source paper \(p_s\) cites target paper \(p_t\). A provider observation may initially identify one endpoint only by its Semantic Scholar identifier, but at least one endpoint must already be local. Persisting that unresolved observation preserves information that can be repaired when the external identity is subsequently associated with a local paper. Self-edges and duplicates are removed during resolution, and only edges whose endpoints resolve to local paper UUIDs enter the public graph.

The public graph query performs a bounded bidirectional traversal from the selected paper. Depth is restricted to one or two hops, and the node limit cannot exceed 200. Citation-derived weighting, deterministic label propagation, and weighted-degree ranking enrich the bounded snapshot; the public service does not inject author or concept nodes and does not supply keyword co-occurrence input. If enrichment fails, chronological citation ordering provides a deterministic fallback and the response marks the algorithm status. Persisting raw citation observations but computing the enriched view per request avoids invalidation rules for stored layouts, at the cost of repeated computation over the bounded neighborhood.

Across document, behavior, and graph paths, the same reliability rule applies: durable domain state precedes replaceable execution or presentation state. Stable identifiers make retries safe; model, parser, behavior, and graph versions prevent silent semantic changes; and public responses expose bounded status without returning internal exception text. Paper excerpts sent to model providers exclude raw behavioral history and unnecessary profile fields. Secrets remain outside source and mobile resources, while generated-artifact metadata records enough configuration to interpret a result. These mechanisms establish an inspectable research system, but their effectiveness, latency, and quality remain empirical questions addressed by the tests in Chapter~4.

\section{API Design}
\label{sec:api-design}

The API is the boundary at which the mobile experience, durable domain model, and background engine must agree on meaning. Mneme uses a resource-oriented JSON contract under \texttt{/v1}, with \texttt{snake\_case} fields, typed UUID identities, and ISO~8601 timestamps. FastAPI generates the runtime OpenAPI~3.1.0 schema, while a frozen OpenAPI~3.0.3 document serves as the compatibility specification against which the public surface is checked. The interface contains 13 operations rather than exposing internal worker stages as ad hoc remote procedures. Papers, preferences, briefings, questions, events, graph neighborhoods, and jobs each have a public representation that can be generated from the application and checked against that compatibility specification. This contract-first arrangement lets the Android client map network objects into its own domain models without depending on database tables or model-provider payloads.

Table~\ref{tab:api-surface} groups the operations by responsibility. The unauthenticated health operation establishes only that the application process can answer an HTTP request; it does not certify PostgreSQL, Redis, or external-provider availability. Protected operations use a pre-provisioned principal so that user-scoped resources retain explicit ownership even in a single-user demonstration. Collection endpoints use opaque keyset cursors and a default page size of 20, bounded between 1 and 50. The client consequently cannot derive ordering assumptions from the cursor representation, and insertion of a newer paper or briefing does not make an offset silently refer to a different record.

Idempotency is expressed differently for the two write patterns. Pipeline creation derives a uniqueness key from canonical stage inputs, so repeated summary requests converge on one logical job. Behavioral ingestion instead accepts client-generated event UUIDs and reports how many items were newly accepted and how many were duplicates. The entire event batch and behavior recomputation execute transactionally for one user. Recalculation derives the positive and negative profile embeddings, bounded confidence, and evidence summary from the authoritative event history without exposing those persistence fields through the ingestion response. If any referenced paper or timestamp violates the contract, the batch is rejected rather than partially changing the profile. These semantics let the mobile client retain an event until acknowledgement and retry after uncertain connectivity without needing to infer whether the first request reached the server.

The question endpoint is synchronous because its useful response is the bounded answer itself. A request identifies one paper, an optional conversation, and a question of at most 500 characters. The conversation UUID preserves ownership and paper scope, but generation remains grounded in the new question and selected revision rather than treating unbounded chat history as evidence. The response contains public source records at section and chunk identity granularity; it does not claim character-level spans or expose internal prompt context. Similarly, the graph endpoint exposes paper nodes and directed citation edges but not provider-specific observation records. These transformations prevent persistence details from becoming accidental public promises.

Every failure crosses the API through one envelope containing \texttt{code}, \texttt{message}, \texttt{request\_id}, and optional structured \texttt{details}. Validation details describe locations and error types without echoing rejected values. Database and unexpected failures are logged with the request identity but become non-sensitive service or internal errors at the boundary. The same sanitized \texttt{X-Request-ID} is attached to the response, giving the client a support handle without exposing stack traces. Stable application codes distinguish, for example, an unknown paper, an unavailable queue, invalid pagination, inadequate configuration, and malformed input even when several cases share an HTTP status.

All operations except process health require an opaque Bearer token representing the pre-provisioned user. The raw token is supplied from deployment and mobile configuration rather than source resources; the server stores its SHA-256 digest and compares the received token in constant time. This mechanism intentionally solves only the bounded identity requirement of the study system. It is not a registration, recovery, revocation, or multi-user authorization design, and the thesis does not treat it as a security contribution. Transport encryption, secret rotation, per-user credentials, and stronger authorization remain deployment obligations beyond this narrow interface.

Three alternatives clarify the contract's priorities. Blocking until document processing finishes would produce a simpler response type but would couple mobile timeouts to PDF and model latency; the explicit job resource was preferred. Offset pagination would be easier to inspect manually but is unstable under insertion; opaque keyset cursors were preferred. Returning database or provider objects directly would reduce mapping code but would leak storage evolution and sensitive diagnostics into the app; explicit Pydantic response schemas and a common error envelope were preferred. The added translation code is therefore a deliberate cost paid for compatibility, failure clarity, and a narrower privacy surface.

\section{UI/UX Design}
\label{sec:ui-ux-design}

The interaction design preserves the story map's research loop while reducing the number of decisions required on a phone. A conventional document application often begins with search or a library; Mneme begins with a briefing because the principal recurring problem is deciding what deserves attention next. Search remains an alternate entry for a known paper, but it does not displace the personalized queue. Figure~\ref{fig:ui-flow} shows the resulting information architecture. Onboarding establishes a small explicit interest profile and one seed reference, the briefing supports triage, paper detail supports understanding, and Q\&A and graph views branch from a paper whose identity is already visible. The stable identity carried by each route lets those views refer to one paper record while retaining their own typed presentation state. Interest editing and observed interactions feed subsequent briefing generation rather than interrupting the current reading task.

The onboarding path follows progressive disclosure. The first screen explains the product in terms of reading outcomes, after which the user chooses a small number of research areas and supplies an arXiv reference. The seed does not purport to recover a complete preference profile. It gives the interface enough context to demonstrate a briefing and leaves the explicit topics available for correction. This decision avoids both an empty first session and a long account questionnaire. It also follows the human--AI interaction principle that a system should communicate what it can do and make correction possible when an inferred result is wrong~\cite{amershi2019guidelines}.

The briefing screen is organized for rapid comparison rather than for complete paper reading. Each card presents title, authorship, category or date context, a short description, a bounded relevance score, and a concise reason before the primary open action. Recommendation rationale is positioned near the score because a detached explanation is unlikely to be used during triage. Saving and preference tuning remain secondary actions so they do not compete with opening the paper. An empty briefing preserves the navigation and offers search or interest correction; it does not fabricate recommendations to keep the screen visually populated.

Paper detail changes the information density. The title and source identity remain at the top, while the generated account separates overview, claims, method, and limitations. Citation chips and source-match labels sit adjacent to the text they qualify. Each source record exposes its section context and the canonical paper source; selecting it follows that source link rather than implying that the public client can retrieve an exact stored excerpt. This placement responds to the customer requirement that a useful summary must support verification, while the labels avoid implying that local source matching establishes scientific correctness. A partial or abstract-only representation uses text as well as colour to disclose its evidence tier.

The Q\&A screen inherits the selected paper identity and asks a question within that scope. The answer body and source list are presented as one inspection unit; source markers are not hidden behind a generic ``AI answer'' card. A matched, partial, or insufficient-evidence label describes the bounded post-generation check defined in Section~\ref{sec:engine-architecture}. Conversation identity allows related exchanges to be grouped, but the interface does not describe the service as corpus-wide synthesis or assume that earlier turns supplied evidence to a later answer. When no adequate excerpt is found, the refusal occupies the normal answer region, keeping evidence insufficiency distinct from a transport failure.

Figures~\ref{fig:prototype-reading} and~\ref{fig:prototype-graph} document the interactive prototype used to test these decisions. All titles, recommendations, scores, summaries, answers, citations, graph nodes, and actions in these captures are controlled fixtures. The figures are therefore evidence of screen hierarchy and task affordances, not of live ingestion, ranking quality, model correctness, or network integration.

Graph exploration uses a locally packaged WebView because a force-directed canvas and interaction bridge are more tractable in Web technology than in a custom Compose drawing stack. The server response remains a layout-independent node-and-edge object. Android serializes that object for the local renderer, while JavaScript computes the presentation. The narrow return bridge rejects an identifier that is absent from the currently displayed graph and posts an accepted paper UUID to the Android main thread. The native layer then preserves the selection, mirrors the title and relationship context outside the canvas, and supplies the \emph{Open Paper} action. This boundary keeps navigation and accessibility semantics under Android control while allowing graph presentation to evolve independently from server-side graph construction, clustering, and ranking.

The graph requires stronger affordance than a conventional list because spatial proximity does not itself tell a first-time user what is actionable. The design therefore places a short instruction above the canvas, gives the selected node a persistent visual state, explains why the selected paper is related, and keeps an \emph{Open Paper} action visible after selection. Edge direction and algorithmic grouping require a legend, and node labels are truncated visually without replacing the full title exposed to the native preview. The paper-only API is narrower than the prototype in Figure~\ref{fig:prototype-graph}, whose concept and author tabs explored an interface alternative; those prototype tabs are not evidence that heterogeneous nodes belong to the public graph schema.

Network state is treated as interface content rather than as an implementation detail. On opening a briefing, the client can restore the latest local snapshot and then request a live one. The visible origin distinguishes restored cache from a server response, and a refresh failure leaves the last valid snapshot readable. Paper Q\&A and graph exploration have no semantically equivalent local substitute, so loss of connectivity produces a retryable state rather than a controlled answer or graph disguised as live data. Processing is likewise distinct from failure: an HTTP~202 response shows the paper stage and job state, whereas a validation or service error uses the stable message and request identity from the API envelope.

The minimum behavioral capture policy follows the same restraint. Impression, paper-open, and paper-scoped-question events are attached to stable paper identities, stored locally until acknowledgement, and uploaded in bounded batches. An impression records exposure rather than interest, while an open or paper-scoped question supplies positive evidence whose eventual influence is moderated by profile confidence. The interface neither displays a claim that the system has learned a user's intent nor sends raw reading history to the language-model prompt. Instead, recommendation reasons expose the components that affected the score, and explicit topics remain editable. This makes personalization observable enough to contest without requiring the user to understand embedding vectors.

The visual system uses a restrained navy, teal, gold, green, and red palette consistent with the diagrams in this chapter, but no state relies on hue alone. Text labels, icons, selected outlines, and action placement carry the operational meaning. Large targets, scalable text, semantic content descriptions, predictable back navigation, and a native selected-node summary are design requirements for touch and screen-reader use. These mechanisms support accessibility but do not by themselves establish conformance or usability; the formative task study below evaluates discoverability of the principal flow, while broader accessibility testing belongs to the empirical assessment in Chapter~4.

\section{Usability Testing}
\label{sec:usability-testing}

The architecture and interaction design establish what the system should expose, but they do not show whether a first-time reader can discover the intended path. A formative usability study therefore examined the interactive prototype with five participants tested individually. The study focused on comprehension and navigation across the central research loop rather than account creation or model quality. Its evidence consists of task completion counts and qualitative observations from moderated sessions. Because the prototype used fixed content, the study evaluates whether a participant could locate and act on an explanation, source, answer, graph node, or preference control; it does not evaluate whether the underlying recommendation or generated answer was correct.

\subsection{Usability Testing Script}
\label{subsec:usability-script}

Each session began with the same explanation: the interface, rather than the participant, was under test; confusing behavior was useful evidence; participation could stop at any time; and the participant should think aloud. A test identity was already available so that routine setup would not consume the session. The facilitator presented tasks without naming a screen or control and responded to requests for help with non-directive prompts rather than instruction. This procedure was intended to preserve evidence of false starts and hesitation, particularly for controls whose appearance suggested information but not action.

The script's research questions matched the story-map transitions. It asked whether a participant could understand why a briefing item was recommended, recover a paper's main contribution and one source, ask a paper-scoped question and inspect the cited evidence, move from a paper into related work through the graph, and correct the interest profile. The pre-session questions covered paper-reading frequency, research area, discovery practice, prior AI-reading tools, and familiarity with citation graphs. The post-session questions asked which component was useful or confusing, whether reasons and citations affected confidence, and what should change. These questions contextualized observation, but the retained study record does not contain participant-level questionnaire responses; they are therefore not converted into frequencies or psychometric claims.

Table~\ref{tab:usability-script} defines the five tasks and the observable success criterion used in analysis. Target time and action counts were specified before testing as facilitation guides. The study record preserves success counts but not participant-level completion time, tap count, or hesitation duration, so those targets are not reported as measured outcomes. T3 is operationalized as locating an answer and its cited excerpt rather than as ``trusting'' the answer, because trust is not established by task completion alone.

The 15 of 15 completions across T1--T3 support a limited interface conclusion: under explicit task prompts, participants could move from a recommendation reason to paper detail, locate a source-oriented summary path, and inspect evidence associated with a Q\&A response. Session notes described the briefing as readily legible and citation-backed answers as useful. They also indicated that verification deserved a stronger visual cue. The apparent tension is informative. A participant who is explicitly asked to verify a source may locate it, while a reader pursuing an ordinary task may not notice the same control spontaneously. The design response is therefore to retain the briefing structure while placing source labels and citation chips closer to the claims they qualify. These observations do not measure factual accuracy or a calibrated change in trust.

T4 produced the only majority failure. Three participants did not infer which graph elements could be selected or how selection led to a paper. The visual form attracted attention, but node clickability and the next action were not self-evident. This finding motivated three coupled changes: an instruction cue states that a node can be tapped, the selected node acquires a persistent state with relationship context, and an \emph{Open Paper} action remains visible after selection. These changes address the observed failure mechanism more directly than changing graph layout alone. Because the study record contains no subsequent test of the revised interaction, they are reported as evidence-based design decisions rather than as demonstrated improvements.

T5 was completed by four participants. The single failure and accompanying observation indicated that interest controls were understandable once found but not sufficiently prominent. Preference tuning therefore remains one action away from the briefing and receives an explicit settings entry. This placement also supports the broader transparency objective: a user who sees an irrelevant reason can inspect and correct explicit interests without leaving the reading loop. The study does not establish whether the correction changes later recommendation quality; that causal question belongs to the system evaluation rather than to navigation testing.

Several threats constrain interpretation. The sample contains five participants, and the retained record does not provide recruitment method, demographics, research experience by participant, or a basis for inferential sampling claims. Moderated prompts can increase feature awareness, particularly when a task explicitly names a graph or source. The prototype contains static fixtures and simulated actions, so completion does not establish network reliability, ranking relevance, retrieval quality, generation faithfulness, or mobile performance. Participant-level time, tap, hesitation, questionnaire, and free-response data were not retained in a form suitable for analysis. Finally, the graph revisions were not retested. The appropriate conclusion is therefore that the study identified a legible reading spine and a concentrated graph-discoverability defect; broader effectiveness claims require the independent technical and user-facing evaluations specified in Chapter~4.
